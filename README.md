# My Programming Journey

I have been programming in a rudimentary form since the original Apple IIe that was available in both my elementray school and the local public library.  Initially this manifested as simple loops displaying repeating text over and over on a constant scroll, but that quickly became boring and I moved on to more complex ideas.  In fifth grade (during Desert Shield/Storm), a frend and I started working on a game revolving around a military mission to defeat Saddam Hussein.  Ultimately this became more of a series of graphics depicting the view from the cockpit of a jet fighter flying over a desert landscape, on the way to defeat the dictator.

After this attempt, I fell away from programming for a while, until the early 2000s, when I discovered C#.  I taught myself C#/.Net/WPF from a book, and was soon making Windows applications for anything I could think of.  During this time I was working in a collections call center for TD Auto Finance, so I started developing an application to assist my daily workload.  Each night I would come home with a new list of features I had thought of throughout the evening to add to the app, and before long I had a diverse application that helped to take notes on calls, calculate payments and payoff amounts, and track my collection attempts from one day to the next.  Eventually I presented this app to the Collection Department VPs, and this actually led to my first IT position, running the automated dialer system for TDAF.  There was also a plan to deploy the app companywide, but as with many things in business this got stalled in various committees.

Once I left this position, I needed another application to work on to hone my programming skills, so I began working on an app to track the games in my Xbox library.  This is not something that's very easy to do on the console itself, or on Microsoft's Xbox website, and I found myself occasionally coming close to buying another version of a game I already owned.  That app can be found at <a href="https://www.github.com/kmcbarne/XboxLibrary">Xbox Library</a>.
